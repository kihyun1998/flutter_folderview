# Scroll anchoring

## What it is
Keeps what the user is looking at still when the content changes under the viewport: rows inserted or removed above it, a Scale change, or a bulk expand or collapse. The offset math is pure; the widget applies it.

## Governing decisions
No decision record. One maintainer's judgement, made at #205's triage (2026-11-15), governs the row-geometry anchor; see **Row-geometry change** under the design model for what it was decided on.

## Design model
Read from `ScrollAnchor` and `_FolderViewContentState.didUpdateWidget`.
- `ScrollAnchor` only computes offsets and never touches a controller. It returns `null` when nothing needs to move; the Scale and bulk methods also return `null` for a move of 0.5 px or less, against churn. The incremental method has no threshold.
- **Incremental change** (from `FlattenChange`): if the changed row's bottom edge (`topPadding + (index + 1) × itemExtent`) is at or above the current offset, shift by `deltaItems × itemExtent`, clamped to `[0, newContentHeight − viewportHeight]`, the end of the **new** content in the **new** view: `FolderViewContent.viewportHeight`, which `_FolderViewState.build` computes for the layout to come ([pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md)). Otherwise do nothing. Applied at once in `didUpdateWidget`; the vertical bar follows after layout through [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)'s metrics re-follow. A change is applied once, in the update that brought it: when the new widget's `change` is the same object as the old one's, the branch does nothing. `_FolderViewState.build` computes the flatten result outside its `LayoutBuilder`, so a relayout without a rebuild (new constraints, the same `FolderView`) hands the same `FlattenChange` and flat list again. The guard is the `change`'s identity, not the list's: the Flattener mutates its cached list in place on an incremental change ([flattening](flattening.md)), so the list is the same object before and after a real expand, and a list-identity guard stops every incremental anchor. Each incremental `update` constructs a new `FlattenChange`. Pinned by `test/widgets/folder_view_incremental_anchor_test.dart`, the relayout case included (#193).
- **How the incremental and bulk moves are applied** (`_anchorTo`, #195, #203). While the vertical bar is dragged, the body and the bar are each corrected to the offset with `correctBy`. Otherwise, while a user's scroll runs on the body (`userScrollDirection` not idle: a wheel motion, a touch drag, a fling), the body is corrected by the difference; otherwise it jumps. On a `SmoothScrollController`, `correctBy` also shifts a running wheel motion's position and target by the same amount and keeps its velocity (`_WheelMotionActivity.shift`, `flutter_smooth_wheel_scroll` 0.1.7), so the notch settles at its old target plus the rows added or removed. A `jumpTo` ends the motion, and the rest of the notch went with it: Windows target, 60 Parents of 20 Children, 400×300, default spring, at 20000 a notch of 600 with an expand of the first Parent (+800) three frames in settled at 20854.6, and now at 21400 (Flutter 3.47.1, 2026-11-12).
  - `correctBy` is a layout-time API and notifies no listener. It holds in `didUpdateWidget` because a layout follows in the same frame and reads the value: whenever the anchor moves, the rows in view change index, so the sliver moves its children and the viewport lays out again (read from SDK 3.47.1 `SliverMultiBoxAdaptorElement.performRebuild`). That rests on the rows being keyed by Node `id` ([node-id-identity](../invariant/node-id-identity.md)): with the keys removed, the drag case's rows were not laid out at the corrected offset in the frame of the change (2026-11-12); with the keys kept and `findChildIndexCallback` removed, every case still passed. The correction also makes that layout call the activity's `applyNewDimensions`, which clamps a wheel motion's target to the new end and restarts a fling from the corrected offset. The vertical bar follows on the motion's next tick through the mirror, and through [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)'s metrics re-follow.
  - Not `correctBy` always: a programmatic `animateTo` (`DrivenScrollActivity`) runs to an absolute offset and keeps no shift, so a correction under it is undone on its next tick and the rows move by the change. The jump ends it where the rows stay. `isScrollingNotifier` would include it; `userScrollDirection` is set only by user input and reset to idle by every activity that is not scrolling, so an animation started from rest jumps. At rest the jump also keeps the listeners and scroll notifications it fires, as before.
  - The wheel motion's activity type is private upstream, so the branch cannot be limited to it: a drag and a fling go on through an expand too, whatever `wheelMotion` is. A touch drag of 80 up from 20000, the first Parent expanded, then 100 more ended at 20880 before (the rest of the drag moved nothing) and at 20980 now; a trackpad drag 20900 → 21000; a fling that settles at 20827.4 alone settled at 21042.2 with the expand three frames in, and now at 21627.4.
  - **A thumb drag** (#203). The bar runs the drag and the body follows it through the mirror's `jumpTo`s, so the body's `userScrollDirection` stays idle. A jump of the body is mirrored to the bar with `jumpTo`, which ends the drag; a correction of the body alone is followed by [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)'s metrics re-follow with a `jumpTo`, which ends it too. With the bar corrected as well, the re-follow finds it in place. Before, the rest of the drag moved nothing: Windows target, 60 Parents of 20 Children, 400×300, the thumb pressed near the top and moved down in steps of 10, three steps reached 5869.0 and three more 11738.1; with the first Parent expanded after three, the view went to 6669.0 and stayed there (collapsed from 5964.3: 5164.3 and stayed). Now the expand ends at 11928.6 and the collapse at 11738.1 (Flutter 3.47.1, 2026-11-12).
    - **The bar's drag is read from its notifications, not from its `userScrollDirection`.** `_onBarScroll` listens around each bar: a `ScrollStartNotification` or `ScrollUpdateNotification` with `dragDetails` adds the bar's axis to `_barsDragged`, one without (a fling, a track tap's animation, a jump the mirror makes) removes it, and so does a `ScrollEndNotification`. The incremental and bulk anchors read the vertical one; the Scale anchor reads both (#208). The updates matter because a drag that turns into a fling sends no start or end: `beginActivity` notifies only when `isScrolling` changes (SDK 3.47.1 `ScrollPosition.beginActivity`), and both are scrolling. A fling on the bar is therefore ended where the rows stay, as on main: a correction under it is undone on its next tick when the bar's own view is not laid out again (`BallisticScrollActivity.applyNewDimensions` then never runs). Android target, a touch fling on the bar from 20000, three frames in, the first Parent expanded and the last collapsed in one update (the same content height): with the flag held through the fling the view went 21012.2 → 20241.9 on the next frame and settled at 20797.4, the rows moving back; it now stays at 21012.2 (#203's re-review, 2026-11-12). A touch drag on the bar on Android or iOS, and a trackpad pan over it, set the flag too and go on through a change: on Android, 50 more after an expand at 20850 ended at 20900 (probed 2026-11-12). The direction would miss a drag that has not moved yet: a mouse press on the thumb starts the `DragScrollActivity` at once (SDK 3.47.1 `RawScrollbar` thumb recognizer, `touchSlop` 0, `DragStartBehavior.down`), and the direction is set only by the first non-zero move. At 49300 (the end), the thumb pressed 5 above the view's bottom and pushed down 3 twice, then the first Parent expanded: the view went to 50100 and the drag ended; it now goes on (then two steps of 10 up: 47316.7). Released at 5869.0 and pressed again without moving, the same: it now ends at 11928.6, as a drag of six steps does. `ScrollPosition.activity` would say it directly but is `@protected`, and `isScrollingNotifier` is also true under a track tap's animation, which must jump (below).
    - **A track tap's animation is ended where the rows stay**, as a caller's `animateTo` is: it is a `DrivenScrollActivity` on the bar, toward an absolute offset, and a correction under it is undone on its next tick (with every start counted as a drag, the view ended at the tap's target, 20240.0, instead of the offset mid-animation plus 800; 2026-11-12).
    - **The body alone is corrected during its own scrolls**, as in #195: the bar then follows through the mirror and the re-follow, which is harmless because the bar runs nothing.
    - The drag does not go on by adding each move to the corrected offset. `RawScrollbarState._getPrimaryDelta` (SDK 3.47.1) maps the pointer's distance from the drag's start onto the track as it is now, so the next move puts the thumb back under the pointer: after the expand, three further steps moved 5259.5 against 5869.0 with no change, and the end is where six steps from the top land with every Parent expanded. When that target is behind the current offset (the branch upstream keeps for a shrinking extent), the move since the last update is added instead: ten Parents expanded at once after three steps went to 13011.9, and three further steps moved 5964.3, as three steps from the top do with every Parent expanded. The drag's start is private upstream, so continuing by delta would take a scrollbar of our own that restarts the drag on each anchor. The maintainer took upstream's mapping over the issue's first acceptance text (an advance of ≈ 5869 from 6669), shown the measurements above (#203, 2026-11-12).

Always `jumpTo` kills the five wheel cases, both drags and the fling; for #203 (2026-11-12), main's `_anchorTo`, never taking the bar branch, or leaving the bar out of the correction kills the four thumb-drag cases (the bar's activity is idle after the change); reading the bar's `userScrollDirection` instead of the flag kills the pressed-not-moved case; counting every scroll start as a drag kills the case of a change in the frame a track tap starts (its first tick does not move, so no update has cleared the flag yet); ignoring scroll updates kills the bar-fling case; counting every update as a drag kills it and the mid-animation track-tap case; an end that does not clear the flag kills the after-the-drag case (no listener hears a jump); correcting the body at rest instead of jumping kills that case and the programmatic-animation one; dropping the body branch kills the wheel, drag and fling cases. Nothing drives the `hasClients` check: the flag could only outlive the bar if the bar left mid-drag, and every move of the body afterwards mirrors a jump to the bar, whose start notification clears it. A change can take the bar away mid-drag: a collapse, or a Scale change that turns `needsVerticalScroll` or `needsHorizontalScroll` off (#208). Probed by #208's review (a label of 17 characters, and 12 rows, each with Scale 1.5 → 1.0 during the drag): the bar went, nothing threw, the body was corrected to 0, and the release threw nothing either. - **Row-geometry change** (#205): a change of the row extent (`rowHeight + rowSpacing`) or the top padding between the old and new `FolderViewContent.theme` anchors as a Scale change does, whatever caused it. `theme` is already the scaled theme, so comparing old and new covers a Scale change, a caller's theme change, and both in one update. `didUpdateWidget` runs `_applyRowGeometryAdjustment` when the Scale or that geometry changed; the rows' anchor is then computed in the old theme, as in #202 below, and the vertical mapping is `offsetsForScaleChange`'s. A theme change that keeps the extent and the top padding (colours, a `rowHeight` 30 + `rowSpacing` 10 against 40 + 0) takes the rows-only path and moves nothing.
  - **Inside the top padding the offset keeps its share of the padding**, not a share of a row (`offsetsForScaleChange`, #205). The row mapping alone measured the stretch above row 0 in rows, which holds for a Scale change, where the padding and the extent scale by the same factor, and not for a theme change of the extent alone: with a top padding of 30, `rowHeight` 40 → 20 moved the view from 0 to 15 and from 10 to 20, and 40 → 60 from 10 to 0, where main had kept the rows still (#205's lens pass, probed 2026-11-15). For a Scale change the two mappings give the same offset, so its behaviour is unchanged. A derivation, not the maintainer's call. At an offset equal to the old padding both give the new padding, so `<` against `<=` is not observable (that mutation survives). With no old padding the branch does not run, which keeps an overscroll above 0 from dividing by zero.
    - **A list at its top when the top padding grows** (maintainer, at #205's close: kept). From 0 with `contentPadding.top` 0 → 30 the view goes to 30: the rows stay still and the new padding is scrolled out of sight. That follows the rows rule, and the first row stays at the top of the view. The maintainer kept it over leaving a list at its top at 0, shown this lens finding (not reproduced by the conductor) and the alternative; theirs to reverse (#205's lens pass).
  - **The maintainer's judgement, not a derivation** (#205 triage, 2026-11-15). Two alternatives were put to them: anchor any extent or top-padding change like a Scale change, or leave a theme change on the pixel offset, as a plain `ListView` does. They chose the anchor, because `FolderView` does not expose its vertical controller, so a caller cannot correct the position themselves. They were shown the triage probe: 60 Parents of 20 Children, tree mode, 400×300, at 20000, `rowHeight` 40 → 60 alone stayed at 20000 with the top row `p23-c16` → `p15-c17`, and with the first Parent expanded in the same update went to 21200 where 31200 keeps the rows still.

  - **During a thumb drag** (#208), on the axis whose bar is dragged, the body and the bar are each corrected to the mapped offset in `didUpdateWidget`, before layout, and that axis takes no post-frame jump; the other axis jumps post-frame as before. In the post-frame callback this frame's layout is done, so a `correctBy` there would be drawn only on the next layout, and a notifying move reaches the bar through the mirror's `jumpTo`. - **A Scale change and rows added or removed above the view in one update** (#202): the rows' anchor is computed first, in the **old** Scale (`_rowsAnchorOffset` handed the old theme's `itemExtent` and top padding) and **not clamped to the end**, and nothing moves yet; the Scale anchor then maps that offset instead of the current one and clamps it to the true end, in its post-frame jump. One clamp, at the end: the viewport does not scale, so an end clamp in the old Scale is not the end in the new one, and the distance it cut is not restored by the mapping. A first draft clamped to the new list's end in the old Scale: at the end (50100), collapsing p0 and p59 together with Scale 1.0 → 1.5 ended at 72750 against a new end of 72900, p59 off screen; main reached 72900 (lens probe and test, 2026-11-12). Only a bulk change can reach that clamp: an incremental change above the view shifts the offset and the end by the same rows. The result is the row anchor followed by the Scale anchor. The rows' move cannot be taken in the new Scale on the old offset: the offset is in old-Scale pixels, and the shift would be `deltaItems × newItemExtent` added before the mapping scales it again (expanding 20 rows from 20000 with Scale 1.0 → 1.5 would give 31800, not 31200). Nor can the Scale callback read the offset after layout: a layout at a smaller Scale clamps the old offset to the new, shorter end before the callback reads it (every Parent expanded, at 60000 with Scale 1.5 → 1.0, the callback read 50100, the new end; probed 2026-11-12). `offsetsForScaleChange` returns `vertical: null` for a move of 0.5 px or less relative to the offset it was handed, so the jump then falls back to the rows' offset; otherwise a Scale change too small to move the view would drop the rows' move (Scale 1.0 → 1.0005 with an expand at 40 stayed at 40, where ~840.4 keeps the rows still). Pinned by `test/widgets/folder_view_scale_with_rows_anchor_test.dart`: expand, collapse and a two-Parent bulk expand, each with Scale 1.0 → 1.5 and 1.5 → 1.0, the small-Scale case, an expand with Scale 1.0 → 1.5 under a top padding of 30 (the padding scales with the rest), and the bulk collapse at the end. Measured on main before the fix (Windows target, 60 Parents of 20 Children, 400×300, from row 500): 30000 for 31200 (expand), 30000 for 28800 (collapse), 30000 for 32400 (bulk), and the 1.5 → 1.0 cases all 20000 for 20800 / 19200 / 21600 (2026-11-12). The rows' move here is the Scale path's post-frame `jumpTo`, not `_anchorTo`, so during a user's scroll it ends the motion like any Scale change ([wheel-motion](wheel-motion.md)).
- **Bulk change** (a new flat list without a `FlattenChange`, for example expand-all): anchor to the top node, or to its nearest surviving ancestor if the top node was removed. Skipped when either list is empty. Clamped to the end of the new content in `FolderViewContent.viewportHeight`, as the incremental branch is; the vertical bar follows after layout ([pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md)). Pinned by `test/widgets/folder_view_scrollbar_wheel_test.dart`.
- All three read `itemExtent` as `rowHeight + rowSpacing` of the theme they are handed ([row-extent](../invariant/row-extent.md)). The incremental and bulk anchors are handed the new theme, or the old one when the Scale, the row extent or the top padding changes in the same update.
- A View Mode switch does not anchor; the controllers are recreated at 0, by design ([scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)).

## Reference behaviour
Flutter SDK 3.47.1 `RawScrollbarState` (`widgets/scrollbar.dart`: `handleThumbPressStart`, `_getPrimaryDelta`, the thumb recognizer) and `DragScrollActivity.dispatchScrollStartNotification`, read raw for #203: how a thumb drag maps the pointer and when it reports its start. Not binding; it decides how the drag goes on after the anchor.

## Cross-cutting invariants
- [pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md) — the incremental and bulk anchors run before layout and compute the new end themselves; so does the row-geometry anchor (a Scale or theme change) on an axis whose bar is dragged, with the viewport `_FolderViewState.build` hands down.
- [row-extent](../invariant/row-extent.md) — the anchor math is in units of `rowHeight + rowSpacing`.
- [node-id-identity](../invariant/node-id-identity.md) — the bulk anchor finds the top Node by `id`, and a correction during a user's scroll is laid out in its frame because the keyed rows change index.

## Blast radius
- [flattening](flattening.md) — when `FlattenChange` is emitted decides which branch runs.
- [scale](scale.md) — reads old and new `rowHeight`, `rowSpacing` and top padding from the scaled theme; a caller's theme change reaches the same anchor through them.
- [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md) — the vertical bar follows a jump or a correction through the metrics re-follow there, and a correction during a motion through the mirror on the next tick; a horizontal jump has none. During a thumb drag the anchor corrects the bar itself, and the Scale anchor does so on either axis. A correction fires no listener and no `ScrollUpdateNotification`; the next notification's `pixels` is right, so only a consumer that sums `scrollDelta` drifts.
- [wheel-motion](wheel-motion.md) — the incremental and bulk anchors shift a running wheel motion; the Scale anchor's jump ends it, and so do a theme change of the row extent or top padding and a row change that comes with either.

## Known holes / open
- **No test pins the vertical bar after a Scale change.** The post-frame jump is mirrored against the settled extent, so neither the old re-sync in `_applyScaleAdjustment` nor the metrics re-follow had a case of its own there (re-sync removed, same values at `rowSpacing` 0 and 8, mid-list and at the end, probed 2026-11-07).
- **A change during a thumb drag that moves the offset but not the content height leaves the thumb drawn at its old place until the next move.** Neither correction repaints the bar, and its own view is not laid out again when `contentHeight` is the same. The first Parent expanded and the last collapsed in one update, mid-drag: body and bar 5869.0 → 6669.0 and the drag goes on, but the thumb's centre stayed at y 204.0, where a change of height moved it to 208.3 (Windows target, probed 2026-11-12, #203's lens pass). Before #203 the jump ended the drag and the thumb moved. Not filed.
- **A collapse whose Children hold the view top lands above the Parent.** The incremental shift is `offset + deltaItems × itemExtent`, clamped, with no fallback to the collapsed row as the bulk branch falls back to the nearest surviving ancestor. Collapsing the Parent at row 10, 20 Children of 40, with the view top at 520 (its third Child), gives 520 − 800 → 0, where the Parent is at 400 (read from the code, 2026-11-11). Only a caller changing the Expanded Set can reach it: the Parent is above the view, so it cannot be tapped.
- **An `animateTo` started during a user's scroll would be corrected, and undone.** Starting a scrolling activity does not reset `userScrollDirection`, so a `DrivenScrollActivity` begun while a wheel motion or a drag runs keeps its direction, and an expand during it takes the `correctBy` branch. Read from the code, not probed. It needs a caller driving the body's position, which `FolderView` does not expose, or a keyboard `ScrollAction` (a 100 ms `animateTo`) from a focusable widget inside the list; rows are wrapped in `ExcludeFocus`.
- **The horizontal Scale mapping takes the whole `contentWidth` change as the Scale's.** It scales the offset by new / old `contentWidth`, both captured at the update; `contentWidth` is also measured again on new `data`, a new effective theme, or a view width that moves the 3× ceiling (`_FolderViewState.build`), so one of those in the same update as a Scale change mixes into the ratio. An expand or collapse does not: the width is measured over collapsed Nodes too. Read from the code, not probed (2026-11-12).
