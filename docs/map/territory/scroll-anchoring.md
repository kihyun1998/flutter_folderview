# Scroll anchoring

## What it is
Keeps what the user is looking at still when the content changes under the viewport: rows inserted or removed above it, a Scale change, or a bulk expand or collapse. The offset math is pure; the widget applies it.

## Governing decisions
**None.**

## Design model
Read from `ScrollAnchor` and `_FolderViewContentState.didUpdateWidget`.
- `ScrollAnchor` only computes offsets and never touches a controller. It returns `null` when nothing needs to move; the Scale and bulk methods also return `null` for a move of 0.5 px or less, against churn. The incremental method has no threshold.
- **Incremental change** (from `FlattenChange`): if the changed row's bottom edge (`topPadding + (index + 1) × itemExtent`) is at or above the current offset, shift by `deltaItems × itemExtent`, clamped to `[0, newContentHeight − viewportHeight]`, the end of the **new** content ([pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md)). Otherwise do nothing. Applied at once in `didUpdateWidget`; the vertical bar follows after layout through [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)'s metrics re-follow. A change is applied once, in the update that brought it: when the new widget's `change` is the same object as the old one's, the branch does nothing. `_FolderViewState.build` computes the flatten result outside its `LayoutBuilder`, so a relayout without a rebuild (new constraints, the same `FolderView`) hands the same `FlattenChange` and flat list again. The guard is the `change`'s identity, not the list's: the Flattener mutates its cached list in place on an incremental change ([flattening](flattening.md)), so the list is the same object before and after a real expand, and a list-identity guard stops every incremental anchor. Each incremental `update` constructs a new `FlattenChange`. Pinned by `test/widgets/folder_view_incremental_anchor_test.dart`, the relayout case included (#193).
- **How the incremental and bulk moves are applied** (`_anchorTo`, #195). While a user's scroll runs on the body (`userScrollDirection` not idle: a wheel motion, a touch drag, a fling), the position is corrected by the difference with `correctBy`; otherwise it jumps. On a `SmoothScrollController`, `correctBy` also shifts a running wheel motion's position and target by the same amount and keeps its velocity (`_WheelMotionActivity.shift`, `flutter_smooth_wheel_scroll` 0.1.7), so the notch settles at its old target plus the rows added or removed. A `jumpTo` ends the motion, and the rest of the notch went with it: Windows target, 60 Parents of 20 Children, 400×300, default spring, at 20000 a notch of 600 with an expand of the first Parent (+800) three frames in settled at 20854.6, and now at 21400 (Flutter 3.47.1, 2026-11-12).
  - `correctBy` is a layout-time API and notifies no listener. It holds in `didUpdateWidget` because a layout follows in the same frame and reads the value: whenever the anchor moves, the rows in view change index, so the sliver moves its children and the viewport lays out again (read from SDK 3.47.1 `SliverMultiBoxAdaptorElement.performRebuild`). That rests on the rows being keyed by Node `id` ([node-id-identity](../invariant/node-id-identity.md)): with the keys removed, the drag case's rows were not laid out at the corrected offset in the frame of the change (2026-11-12); with the keys kept and `findChildIndexCallback` removed, every case still passed. The correction also makes that layout call the activity's `applyNewDimensions`, which clamps a wheel motion's target to the new end and restarts a fling from the corrected offset. The vertical bar follows on the motion's next tick through the mirror, and through [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)'s metrics re-follow.
  - Not `correctBy` always: a programmatic `animateTo` (`DrivenScrollActivity`) runs to an absolute offset and keeps no shift, so a correction under it is undone on its next tick and the rows move by the change. The jump ends it where the rows stay. `isScrollingNotifier` would include it; `userScrollDirection` is set only by user input and reset to idle by every activity that is not scrolling, so an animation started from rest jumps. At rest the jump also keeps the listeners and scroll notifications it fires, as before.
  - The wheel motion's activity type is private upstream, so the branch cannot be limited to it: a drag and a fling go on through an expand too, whatever `wheelMotion` is. A touch drag of 80 up from 20000, the first Parent expanded, then 100 more ended at 20880 before (the rest of the drag moved nothing) and at 20980 now; a trackpad drag 20900 → 21000; a fling that settles at 20827.4 alone settled at 21042.2 with the expand three frames in, and now at 21627.4.
  - Pinned by `test/widgets/folder_view_anchor_wheel_motion_test.dart`; the bulk branch through its two-Parents case, since the Flattener hands a change of two Nodes to a full rebuild. Always `jumpTo` kills the five wheel cases, both drags and the fling; always `correctBy`, or `isScrollingNotifier` as the predicate, kills the programmatic-animation case; `== ScrollDirection.reverse` as the predicate kills the upward notch. A bulk collapse of every Parent during a notch to the end settled on the new end under every one of those mutations (target + shift past the new end is clamped by upstream either way), so it has no test. For an incremental change above the view the target cannot pass the new end: the shift is the full row delta, and the old target is at most the old end.
- **Scale change**: map the top fractional item index into the new item extent; the horizontal offset scales by the new/old `contentWidth` ratio. Applied post-frame (a `jumpTo` during build would `setState` during build), after layout, so the offset mirror already clamps the vertical bar to the new end; the metrics re-follow has nothing left to move.
- **Bulk change** (a new flat list without a `FlattenChange`, for example expand-all): anchor to the top node, or to its nearest surviving ancestor if the top node was removed. Skipped when either list is empty. Clamped to the end of the new content; the vertical bar follows after layout ([pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md)). Pinned by `test/widgets/folder_view_scrollbar_wheel_test.dart`.
- All three read `itemExtent` as `rowHeight + rowSpacing` of the theme they are handed ([row-extent](../invariant/row-extent.md)).
- A View Mode switch does not anchor; the controllers are recreated at 0, by design ([scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md)).

## Reference behaviour
**None.**

## Cross-cutting invariants
- [pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md) — the incremental and bulk anchors run before layout and compute the new end themselves.
- [row-extent](../invariant/row-extent.md) — the anchor math is in units of `rowHeight + rowSpacing`.
- [node-id-identity](../invariant/node-id-identity.md) — the bulk anchor finds the top Node by `id`, and a correction during a user's scroll is laid out in its frame because the keyed rows change index.

## Blast radius
- [flattening](flattening.md) — when `FlattenChange` is emitted decides which branch runs.
- [scale](scale.md) — reads old and new `rowHeight`, `rowSpacing` and top padding from the scaled theme.
- [scroll-sync](scroll-sync.md) — the vertical bar follows a jump or a correction through the metrics re-follow there, and a correction during a motion through the mirror on the next tick; a horizontal jump has none. A correction fires no listener and no `ScrollUpdateNotification`; the next notification's `pixels` is right, so only a consumer that sums `scrollDelta` drifts.
- [wheel-motion](wheel-motion.md) — the incremental and bulk anchors shift a running wheel motion; the Scale anchor's jump ends it.

## Known holes / open
- **No test pins the vertical bar after a Scale change.** The post-frame jump is mirrored against the settled extent, so neither the old re-sync in `_applyScaleAdjustment` nor the metrics re-follow had a case of its own there (re-sync removed, same values at `rowSpacing` 0 and 8, mid-list and at the end, probed 2026-11-07).
- **A collapse whose Children hold the view top lands above the Parent.** The incremental shift is `offset + deltaItems × itemExtent`, clamped, with no fallback to the collapsed row as the bulk branch falls back to the nearest surviving ancestor. Collapsing the Parent at row 10, 20 Children of 40, with the view top at 520 (its third Child), gives 520 − 800 → 0, where the Parent is at 400 (read from the code, 2026-11-11). Only a caller changing the Expanded Set can reach it: the Parent is above the view, so it cannot be tapped.
- **An `animateTo` started during a user's scroll would be corrected, and undone.** Starting a scrolling activity does not reset `userScrollDirection`, so a `DrivenScrollActivity` begun while a wheel motion or a drag runs keeps its direction, and an expand during it takes the `correctBy` branch. Read from the code, not probed. It needs a caller driving the body's position, which `FolderView` does not expose, or a keyboard `ScrollAction` (a 100 ms `animateTo`) from a focusable widget inside the list; rows are wrapped in `ExcludeFocus`.
- **A drag at the end through an anchor and a taller view in one frame is held past the end until release.** The anchor takes the end from the old viewport ([pre-layout-extent](../invariant/pre-layout-extent.md)), and a correction under a drag is pulled back by nothing: `DragScrollActivity.applyNewDimensions` does nothing, and `RangeMaintainingScrollPhysics` does not enforce the boundary once the pixels changed. Windows target, 60 Parents of 20 Children: a touch drag from the end (49300) to 49270, then the first Parent expanded and the view 300 → 500 in one frame, left the body at 50070 against an end of 49900 (the bar at 49900) while held, and at 49900 on release; with a `jumpTo` it was back at 49903.1 within 500 ms while held (2026-11-12, #195). A wheel motion is clamped by upstream's tick. Left as is by the maintainer (#195): it takes all three in one frame, and a release ends it.
