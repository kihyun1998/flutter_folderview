# A scroll extent read before layout is the old one

## The fact
In `didUpdateWidget`, and anywhere else in the build phase, a `ScrollPosition`'s `maxScrollExtent` and `viewportDimension` still describe the last layout, not the content and viewport this frame will lay out. A move made there takes its end from the new content height (`FolderViewContent.contentHeight`, which `SizeService.calculateContentHeight` computes over the new flat list), minus the viewport, not from the position. A controller that follows the jump, clamping to its own extent, is clamped to its old end, so it has to follow again once the frame's layout has moved that end.

## Why it is cross-cutting
Four sites move or clamp a scroll offset around a content change, and one re-follows after layout:
- `_FolderViewContentState._rowsAnchorOffset`, incremental branch: runs in `didUpdateWidget`, clamps to the end of `contentHeight` in `FolderViewContent.viewportHeight`, the viewport `_FolderViewState.build` computes for the coming layout (#210); with a Scale change in the same update it is handed no end and does not clamp.
- The same method, bulk branch: the same, through `ScrollAnchor.verticalOffsetForBulkChange`.
- `_FolderViewContentState._applyRowGeometryAdjustment` (a change of the row extent or top padding, from a Scale or a theme): defers the whole computation to a post-frame callback, so the extents it reads there are already the new ones. The vertical offset it maps is captured before layout: the rows' anchor offset when rows changed in the same update, else the current one. Read after layout with a smaller row extent, it would already be clamped to the new end. On an axis whose scrollbar is dragged it corrects in `didUpdateWidget` instead (#208), and clamps with the new content and the viewport `_FolderViewState.build` computes for the coming layout (`FolderViewContent.viewportWidth` and `viewportHeight`), not with the positions' extents.
- `_SyncedScrollControllersState._jumpToNoCascade`: mirrors a jump to the paired controller clamped to that controller's extent. Called during a build, that extent is the old one.
- `_SyncedScrollControllersState._onScrollMetrics`: on a vertical `ScrollMetricsNotification` from a scroll view directly below `SyncedScrollControllers` (the rows' `ListView` or the vertical bar's own view), moves the bar to the body's offset clamped to the bar's extent, through `_verticalBarFollowsBody`. The notification is dispatched after the frame in which the extents changed, so the bar follows a pre-layout jump without the jump site doing anything (#194).

During a user's scroll the two anchor branches correct the position (`correctBy`) instead of jumping (#195). A correction notifies no listener, so the mirror does not follow it at once; the motion's next tick, or the metrics re-follow, does. During a thumb drag the anchor corrects the bar to the same offset in the same call instead, because that follow would end the drag (#203). The two anchor branches share a method. When the Scale changes in the same update, that method computes in the old Scale, unclamped at the end, and its result is not applied at once but handed to the Scale path, which maps it after layout and clamps it to the true end (#202); on an axis whose bar is dragged, the Scale path maps it before layout instead, with the viewport `_FolderViewState.build` hands down (#208). It clamps only there: an end clamp before the mapping, at the old Scale and the old viewport, cut distance the mapping does not restore. The Scale path and the mirror are separate code that holds the same assumption. No jump site re-syncs the bar itself; the metrics re-follow covers every vertical jump, wherever it is made. All three anchor paths take the end from the new content (checked by reading, 2026-11-11, #176).

The two `didUpdateWidget` branches read the viewport from `FolderViewContent.viewportHeight`, not from the position (#210). The position's `viewportDimension` differs from the new one when the view's own height changes in the same frame, or when the horizontal bar comes or goes in it; the bar depends on `contentWidth > availableWidth` in `_FolderViewState.build`, so on the data, the Scale, the theme and the width. The Flattener takes no theme, so an incremental change can come with a new theme, or a new width, that brings the bar or takes it away. With the old viewport, probed on the Windows target, 60 Parents of 20 Children, 2026-11-11, nothing moved the rows where they could have stayed when nothing was scrolling:
- A bulk change that brought the bar in (400×300): the rows stayed still.
- A bulk change that took it away: the body and the bar both ended on the true end, the rows moving by the 16 px the view grew at the bottom.
- An expand at the end in the same frame as the height going 500 → 300: 49100 → 49900, the rows still. Going 300 → 500: 49300 → 49900, the true end; the target computed from the old viewport was 50100, and layout corrected it.

Layout corrects a jump past the end, not a correction under a user's scroll, and not a jump short of the end: with a taller view, a thumb drag at the end ended, a touch drag was held past the end until release, and a wheel motion was drawn past it for one frame; with a shorter view and a change that cannot keep the rows still, the view stayed short of the new end. With `FolderViewContent.viewportHeight`, each lands on the new end, the bar taken away in the same bulk update included ([scroll-anchoring](../territory/scroll-anchoring.md), #210).

## Territories it holds in
- [scroll-anchoring](../territory/scroll-anchoring.md) — the incremental and bulk anchors run before layout.
- [scroll-sync](../territory/scroll-sync.md) — the mirror clamps to the follower's extent, whatever layout it last had.

## What a violation looks like
The incremental anchor clamped to the position's `maxScrollExtent`. Expanding a Parent of 20 rows of 40 above the view, 60 Parents in a 400×300 view on the Windows target, left 49300 when scrolled to the end and also 100 from it, where keeping the rows still needs 50100 and 50000 (new end 50100). The rows in view moved down by 800 and 700, and the vertical bar stayed with the body at 49300. With the new end but neither a bar re-sync nor the metrics re-follow, those two cases fail on the bar (#176, 2026-11-11), and a bulk expand from the end left 77300 / 6100 (body / bar; 2026-11-07, `flutter_smooth_wheel_scroll` 0.1.6). Turning the metrics re-follow off reddens the same five bar assertions: both expands at the end and at 100 from it, the bulk expand from the end, and the notch after a bulk expand past the old end at once and on every frame (#194, 2026-11-12).

## Discovery history
- The bulk branch's post-frame bar re-sync, already in place when the map was built: the bar reports the old end before layout.
- #176 — the incremental branch clamped to the old end; it now computes the new one as the bulk branch does.
- #194 — the three post-frame bar re-syncs (both anchor branches and the Scale path) gave way to the bar following the body's scroll metrics in `SyncedScrollControllers`.
- #202 — with a Scale change in the same update, the rows' anchor was applied before layout and then overwritten by the Scale path's mapping of the pre-anchor offset; it is now computed in the old Scale and handed to the Scale path.
- #208 — the Scale path's correction during a thumb drag runs before layout; clamped with the positions' old `viewportDimension`, a taller view in the same update at the end put the body past the new end and ended the drag ([scroll-anchoring](../territory/scroll-anchoring.md)). It takes the viewport from `_FolderViewState.build`. The rows' anchor still read the old one, and had that hole during a thumb drag.
- #210 — the rows' anchor takes `FolderViewContent.viewportHeight` too: with the old viewport, a taller view in the same update ended a thumb drag at the end, held a touch drag past the end, and drew a wheel motion past it for one frame.

## Where it will recur
Any jump or clamp made in the build phase in response to new data or a new theme: an "ensure visible" scroll, keeping a selection in view, restoring an offset. Test: does it read `maxScrollExtent` or `viewportDimension` from a position before the frame's layout? If so, it sees the old content. The vertical bar needs nothing from it; a horizontal jump has no such re-follow ([scroll-sync](../territory/scroll-sync.md)).
